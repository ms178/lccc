## Concrete findings and repairs

### R824-1: unchecked rotation-clone affine rewriting

`loop_rotate::canonicalise_affine_exit_cmps` subtracted a constant from a
comparison bound without a no-wrap proof. With the accepted `-fwrapv` option
and `CCC_LOOP_ROTATE=1`, the retained two-trip signed-wrap source returned the
wrong answer. GCC 16.2 and rotation-disabled LCCC returned the expected answer.
`evidence/pr824-review/rotation-before.json` records commands and statuses.

Removed the separate unchecked implementation rather than adding another
exception table. The existing post-rotation affine pass is now the single
owner. It certifies a monotone induction sequence, the first test and the first
failing test in i128 before rewriting. It never needs signed-overflow UB as a
license. The rotation itself remains available under the affine kill switch.

### R824-2: unsigned affine bounds and post-increment reuse

The previous proof admitted signed domains only. Extended that proof to all
supported 8/16/32/64-bit signed and unsigned integer domains; pointer, floating
and 128-bit domains are refused. Constants are normalized in their actual
comparison domain, not interpreted as signed i64 storage patterns.

Additional obligations:

* Comparisons must use the induction variable's domain and continuation
  polarity. Constant start/step/bound and a positive step are required.
* A unique exit test must dominate every loop-closing edge. An optional
  interior test cannot bound iterations that bypass it.
* Non-SSA webs fail closed; a value search is not a reaching-definition proof.
* Same-width additive/Copy chains are followed with a hard depth bound.
  Casts cannot silently erase narrowing or reinterpretation.
* A shared post-increment source may replace the temporary only when both its
  endpoint values and the final sum's endpoint values are representable.
  This preserves the original bare-register compare/branch shape after
  rotation instead of keeping the old phi live across its update.
* Failed plans do not mutate IR; successful plans keep instruction positions,
  source spans and CFG topology unchanged.

A temporary second implementation in IVSR passed 2,394 selected pass tests,
but was discarded: duplicate policy ownership bypassed the existing affine
kill switch and needlessly complicated maintenance. Its intermediate evidence
is labeled as such. The final implementation reuses the existing owner and
restores both rotated and unrotated shape contracts.

The byte-load benchmark's inner loop now compares the IV directly with 4088,
instead of materializing `IV+8` and comparing with 4096. A generic subtraction
without the sequence proof would be wrong near wraparound.

### R824-3: endian-load helper cost before inlining

Reused `byte_load::run_function` before GVN/inlining cost evaluation, keeping
its existing footprint, single-use, side-effect, volatile and target guards.
This exposes a one-load struct-member helper as cheap enough to inline rather
than permanently pricing its original eight-load expression. No inlining
budget was relaxed. RISC-V remains excluded from unaligned widening; x86-64,
i686 and AArch64 retain their established admission.

Also checked the proposed byte-run endpoint before `anchor+k` arithmetic.
Several nonnegative i64 GEP offsets can sum to u64::MAX; a repeated-byte
candidate must refuse rather than overflow an internal span calculation.
Direct negative GEPs remain conservatively unfused; an initial test that
incorrectly expected them to widen was corrected to match that documented
admission, not used as evidence that support had been added.

The early-only candidate improved the bounded byte workload's Ir by about
0.073%, not a dramatic win. Most of the final byte-workload reduction comes
from the proven affine bound, not from relabeling the small early-inline gain.

### R824-4: parity tests had not exercised compiler controls

Both affine shell gates passed `CCC_LOOP_ROTATE`/the affine kill switch to the
**generated executable**, not to its compiler. Their purported configuration
comparisons compiled the same configuration repeatedly. Fixed the environment
placement, propagated report-compilation failures instead of treating them as
zero folds, and added four stub-driven tests of the actual shell functions.
The new cross-target gate is mirrored in hosted/local CI and pinned by a
universal invocation contract. Removing `--cross` from both sides now fails
its mutation test rather than silently reducing coverage.

### R824-5: UTF-8 suffix panic in three x86 parsers

The load/ALU parsers split a mnemonic at byte `len-1` before checking its suffix.
Valid non-ASCII text in an unsupported mnemonic could therefore panic inside
a character. Exact source-body extraction reproduced the slicing panic in the
register and immediate parsers. A shared `strip_suffix('q'/'l')` helper rejects
unsupported text without changing accepted ASCII instructions. The in-place
parser had the same defect and shares the repair. Permanent Rust tests cover
all three; extracted before/after controls are retained. This is a parser-level
robustness result, not a claimed generated-C runtime miscompile.

## Retained PR design choices

Keep the typed vectorization preflight and scalar fallbacks; storage width alone
is not arithmetic-domain equivalence. Keep GVN identity glue separate from raw
use accounting, invariant symbol bases separate from arbitrary value bases, and
RISC-V's addressing costs separate from x86 SIB costs. Keep conservative
implicit register/state effects, low-XMM/YMM-high/ZMM-high distinctions,
write-mask dependencies, pinned-line barriers and stable-symbol checks.
Do not revert semantic protections just to match old instruction counts.

The focused replay passed the native flag/string and implicit-state witnesses,
IVSR struct-walk gate, real nbody semantic census, FMA preflight/side-effect and
long-index controls. The nbody census reported two proven mass-read operands;
that workload-specific count is not a runtime speed claim.

## Performance: distinguish work counts, timing and hardware

The supplied EPYC report determines the priority order, not a claim that this
sandbox is that machine. These native measurements report a Xeon VM, two
allowed CPUs, pinned CPU 0, and no usable PMU measurement. All samples and
outliers are retained.

The selected fifteen's candidate/prior-LCCC simulated-Ir geometric mean is
**0.981991**, arithmetic mean **0.982567**. Best is byte-load reassembly
**0.875016**. The full 45's geomean is **0.992086**, arithmetic mean **0.992403**.
This is less simulated work, not those percentages of physical runtime speedup.
The final fifteen remain behind GCC on the aggregate simulated-Ir metric;
universal compiler superiority is not demonstrated.

A nine-round paired native pilot gave a geomean **1.003226**, arithmetic mean
**1.003460**, with substantial variance. It does **not** establish an overall
runtime improvement. Every pilot median above 1.01 was selected in advance for
31 alternating AB/BA rechecks; Fannkuch was added because of the whole-corpus
counter regression. A separate twenty-call byte workload reduces startup
contamination without changing the kernel's input size or loops.

Important native rechecks (candidate/prior, 95% bootstrap interval):

* Byte workload, twenty calls/process: **0.930505 [0.920588, 0.946193]**.
  This supports a VM-specific improvement for the amplified workload, not an
  EPYC result or a claim about every short process invocation.
* Fannkuch: **0.960401 [0.945631, 0.970682]**, despite higher simulated Ir.
* XML scan: **1.035300 [1.027592, 1.038240]** — a real measured regression in
  this VM screen, not hidden by the aggregate. Disabling the affine fold
  restores the baseline assembly. The fold removes setup-loop work but shifts
  later scratch-register choices/code layout in the hot parser. No proof that
  the instruction reduction is a runtime win is claimed for this workload.
* Switch dispatch's first apparent slowdown was not code causality: its
  generated ELFs are identical, and its recheck interval includes 1. Other
  selected noisy rechecks also retain their full intervals in `summary.json`.

The XML regression is below the standing 5% rejection threshold but above the
2% investigation threshold. Its causal assembly comparison is retained. This
is an explicit unresolved profitability trade-off, not a zero-regression claim.
Avoided a benchmark-name exception or an arbitrary small-loop threshold to hide
it. A broader register-choice stability change needs its own correctness and
performance evidence.

### Rejected policy experiments

The PR's Fannkuch counter regression is **1.042761** versus prior LCCC; it is
present in the original PR and unchanged by this review's additions. A real
separately built SIB-policy-disabled compiler did not recover it, worsened
spectral norm and loop patterns, and failed the existing stencil codegen gate
(+5.7% instructions, +6.7% moves). Disabling inverse strength reduction as well
recovered Fannkuch counter work, but regressed loop patterns **22.5%**, double
reduction **20.7%**, and SHA-256 **14.6%**. Neither experiment ships. The default
policy is retained, not its golden baseline weakened.

## Open acceptance blockers and limits

A newly executed manifestation of existing A14-16 remains open: the driver
silently accepts `-fwrapv`, while a signed product used as an address is widened
unsafely. GCC 16.2 returns the expected result; prior S36, original PR824 and
this candidate all fault. See `accepted-wrap-product-open.json` and its tiny
source. The rotation repair does **not** establish full wrapping-option support.
Giving this option honest semantics requires propagation through the IR and
all overflow-dependent transformations; silently disabling optimization or
claiming the one repaired loop proves the entire mode would be misleading.

Consequently, output matches in the existing `-fwrapv` measurement protocol are
empirical controls, not a proof that every accepted option or UB-sensitive
benchmark is semantically supported. This blocker, the XML profitability loss,
and the wider pre-existing backlog prevent a zero-issues/production-ready claim.
No unsupported approval is attached to the patch.
