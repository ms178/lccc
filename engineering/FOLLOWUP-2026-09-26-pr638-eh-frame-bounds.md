# PR #638 follow-up: red-team of the shared `.eh_frame` reader

**Upstream base:** `8624a5d069f9473adeefdaae26378f69681d8bb4` (`origin/main`, fetched again on 2026-09-27; PR #639). The `.eh_frame` fix began on `a89e685` and was rebased cleanly onto the current base. **Scope:** the newly extended shared ELF `.eh_frame` reader in `src/backend/linker_common/eh_frame.rs`; the x86-64, i686, ARM and RISC-V executable linkers all call its FDE counter/header writer. This is a correctness and input-hardening change, **not** a demonstrated generated-code speedup.

## Reproducer and root cause

A 32-byte input with a DWARF64 length escape (`ff ff ff ff`), an extended length of `u64::MAX`, and a nonzero CIE pointer at byte 12 made `count_eh_frame_fdes` return **1** on the upstream fastbuild although the purported FDE extends far beyond the input. `scan_eh_frame_records` and the header decoder independently computed `body + (length as usize)` without checking overflow; release arithmetic wrapped to an earlier offset. The new unit test `oversized_extended_record_is_not_an_fde` was **RED against upstream** (`left: 1, right: 0`), then GREEN after the change. Separately, unsigned/signed LEB decoders could accept a missing terminator, shift by >=64, and treat bytes in a later record as part of this record. FDE initial-location decoding likewise had only an input-buffer bound, not an **FDE-record** bound. A DWARF64 CIE was recognized by the counter but its `R` augmentation was ignored by the address decoder. A second independent red test, `invalid_cie_pointer_cannot_fabricate_a_header_entry`, showed that a damaged CIE pointer silently fell back to absolute addressing and placed a bogus function address into the header's binary-search table.

## Changes and design rationale

- `record_bounds`: one constant-space structural check shared by the counter, relocated FDE scanner and unrelocated GC scanner. Reject impossible lengths with `usize::try_from`, `checked_add`, per-record ID-width validation and a buffer bound; every accepted record advances strictly. Pass the already-read length through, avoiding redundant loads in the counting path. This is cheaper and less error-prone than independently patching three near-identical scanners.
- Decode the CIE within its own validated boundaries, for both 32- and 64-bit DWARF record formats. Treat a truncated augmentation, misplaced `R` byte or unknown variable-length personality field as **invalid** rather than reading the next CIE. The CIE cache now distinguishes `Some(0)` (valid absolute encoding) from `None` (invalid pointer/CIE) and omits undecodable FDEs instead of inventing search-table entries. Use the version-1 one-byte return-address register rule; newer CIEs use ULEB. Do not change the decoded format of existing ordinary CIEs.
- Bound both LEB readers to at most ten bytes and reject values outside their signed/unsigned 64-bit domains. Pass a slice ending at the **FDE**, not at the enclosing section, to `decode_eh_pointer`. A bad input is not proof of a codegen speedup; the gain is deterministic parsing and safe failure.
- Tests: oversized record and invalid CIE pointer (both red/green), unsigned/signed LEB limits and truncation, valid DWARF64 CIE+FDE pcrel address on both ELF bitnesses, and a deterministic 2,048-case arbitrary-byte/extended-length corpus checking count/scan agreement, monotone in-bounds records and panic-free header builds for 32/64-bit targets. The legacy shared-reader tests and live x86-64/i686 unwinding test must remain green.
